BRICKS / UX DIRECTION 01 / SEPTEMBER 19, 2026

A familiar home.
A clear way into focus.

Keep the physical ritual and useful foundations. Reduce the decisions around them.

This separate HTML/CSS/JavaScript concept uses sample data and simulated NFC and permissions. Production React Native screens and blocking logic have not been edited. Research covers official public product pages and help documentation, not hands-on competitor testing.

Design review: current Bricks app

Overall: Critical text-contrast issues; a useful navigation foundation. The app helps people put distracting apps out of reach with a physical Brick. The physical ritual should define the experience. Statistics, animated status, and repeated cards currently compete with the next action.

Evidence: Home.tsx, FocusLock.tsx, MainTabsNavigator.tsx, Insights.tsx, Settings.tsx, AddBrick.tsx, EmergencyOverride.tsx, and theme/index.ts. This is a code-based review, not a native-device visual audit. Actual TalkBack, font scaling, safe areas, and NFC reliability require device verification.

Critical

Small text lacks contrast. Existing faint text #9AA1AD on white is 2.60:1; dark faint #5A6069 on #181B20 is 2.72:1. These tokens are used for small labels and timestamps. Raise secondary text to at least 4.5:1. accessibility.md › Vision: “Strive to meet color contrast minimum standards.”

Improvements

  • High — Idle Home can imply protection is active. The non-streak pill says “apps blocked” while Home is the inactive state. “Live Activity” also shows the latest session’s records. Use “Not blocking” and “apps selected” before focus; show “Protection on” only when verified. Name past records “Recent sessions.” feedback.md › Best practices: “Consider integrating status feedback into your interface.”
  • High — “Tap a Brick” has two meanings. A screen card starts focus immediately, while scanning a physical device also starts focus. Make the row select a Brick; retain a distinct Start focus action. Use “Scan your phone against Desk Brick” for the physical gesture. This is our design judgment based on current handlers.
  • High — Ending rules require interpretation. Timed, strict, and Brick-only behavior differs. Show the current rule before starting and during focus. A generic End Session label must not unexpectedly lead to a penalty flow. Keep engine rules intact until each variant is agreed and verified against the native service.
  • Medium — Too much emphasis at once. Many labels use 800 weight, with several metric cards, rings, and continuously blinking dots. Use 400–650 for most text and one primary emphasis per screen. typography.md › Conveying hierarchy: “Minimize the number of typefaces you use, even in a highly customized interface.”
  • Medium — Recovery needs a nearby action. The health warning names a permission issue but lacks a recovery button. Add Restore protection, and replace the healthy indicator while protection is interrupted. feedback.md › Best practices: “Show people when a command can’t be carried out and help them understand why.”

What works — retain these

  • Three labeled destinations: Home, Insights, Settings.
  • Personal greeting and saved Brick names grounded in actual places.
  • One shared blocked-app list, matching the current data model.
  • Daily focus progress, a small progress ring, and session history.
  • Physical scanning, timer state, and detection of degraded protection.

Design language and craft

Thesis: a calm, practical home organized around the next physical focus ritual. The small tactile Brick is the signature. System typography and quiet grouped rows carry navigation. Red remains the action color; text and icons make states understandable without relying on hue.

The direction removes the large dashboard hero and duplicate statistic cards, retains one small progress ring, and spends visual emphasis on the Brick and next action. No decorative glass, gradients, invented scores, or looping animation. This is a product-specific interpretation rather than an imitation of a competitor.

Coverage and boundaries

Twelve preview states: ready, active, complete, app selection, Bricks, Insights, Settings, first use, permissions, scanning/retry, interrupted protection, and early ending. Detail screens cover naming, daily goal, ending rules, help, and session history. Search has a no-results state; setup handles an empty name and incomplete permissions.

The clock and history are illustrative. Reload resets prototype state. This direction illustrates timed sessions. Brick-only sessions, strict wait-and-hold accessibility, Android battery optimization, deletion, and native permission denial need a second behavior review before implementation. The simplified early-ending confirmation is explicitly a proposal, not an approved rule change.

Validation before implementation

Ask 3–5 new users to identify whether apps are currently blocked; start with a chosen Brick; find the app list; explain how the session ends; and recover from a disabled permission. Record first-click errors and hesitation. Aim for users to identify state and next action without coaching. A familiar experience depends on predictable locations and recovery, not just polish.

Verify the agreed design on real Android devices: compact width, largest font scale, TalkBack, system navigation, light/dark, missing NFC, revoked permissions, background transitions, and all ending rules. Apply Apple’s principles through React Native and Android conventions rather than imitating iOS permission dialogs.
